=== CDC: INFLUENZA (FLU) ===
TOPIC: Respiratory Disease | Influenza
SOURCE_URL: https://www.cdc.gov/flu
Influenza is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. CDC estimates that flu causes millions of illnesses, hundreds of thousands of hospitalizations, and tens of thousands of deaths in the US annually.
SYMPTOMS: Fever (100–104°F), chills, cough, sore throat, runny/stuffy nose, muscle/body aches, headaches, fatigue. Vomiting and diarrhea more common in children.
EMERGENCY SIGNS IN ADULTS: Trouble breathing, persistent pain/pressure in chest, confusion/altered mental status, inability to keep fluids down, severe or persistent vomiting.
CDC ANTIVIRAL TREATMENT: Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) — 75 mg twice daily x5 days. Zanamivir (Relenza) — inhaled. Peramivir (Rapivab) — single IV dose. Baloxavir (Xofluza) — single oral dose. Start within 48 hours of symptom onset.
CDC PREVENTION: Annual flu vaccination is the best preventive step. All persons 6 months and older should get vaccinated. Frequent handwashing. Avoid close contact with sick individuals.

=== CDC: SEPSIS ===
TOPIC: Critical Care | Sepsis
SOURCE_URL: https://www.cdc.gov/sepsis
At least 1.7 million adults develop sepsis annually in the US. Nearly 270,000 Americans die as a result of sepsis each year.
CDC DEFINITION: Sepsis is the body's extreme response to an infection. It is life-threatening.
SIGNS (GET AHEAD OF SEPSIS): Time matters. Signs include: confusion or disorientation, shortness of breath, high heart rate, fever/shivering/feeling very cold, extreme pain or discomfort, clammy/sweaty skin.
qSOFA SCORE (bedside): Respiratory rate ≥22/min + altered mentation + systolic BP ≤100 mmHg. Score ≥2 = sepsis risk.
SEPTIC SHOCK: Sepsis + need for vasopressor + lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation. Mortality ~40%.
CDC TREATMENT: Hour-1 Bundle — Blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension/lactate ≥4, vasopressors for persistent hypotension.
HIGH-RISK GROUPS: Adults ≥65, children <1 year, chronic conditions (diabetes, lung/kidney/liver disease), weakened immune system.

=== CDC: STROKE ===
TOPIC: Neurological Disease | Stroke
SOURCE_URL: https://www.cdc.gov/stroke
Stroke is the fifth leading cause of death in the US. Someone has a stroke every 40 seconds. About 795,000 people have a stroke each year.
CDC F.A.S.T. RECOGNITION:
F — Face drooping (one side drooping or numb?)
A — Arm weakness (one arm weak or numb?)
S — Speech difficulty (slurred, unable to speak, hard to understand?)
T — Time to call 911 immediately
ADDITIONAL SYMPTOMS: Sudden numbness/weakness in face/arm/leg on one side, sudden confusion, trouble seeing in one/both eyes, sudden trouble walking/dizziness/loss of balance, sudden severe headache with no known cause.
TYPES: Ischemic stroke (87%) — blood clot blocks artery. Hemorrhagic stroke — blood vessel bursts. TIA (mini-stroke) — temporary, no permanent damage but high risk for stroke.
CDC TREATMENT: Ischemic — IV alteplase (tPA) within 4.5 hours, mechanical thrombectomy up to 24 hours. Hemorrhagic — Blood pressure management, surgery.
RISK FACTORS: High blood pressure (leading), smoking, diabetes, high cholesterol, obesity, atrial fibrillation, physical inactivity.

=== CDC: HEART DISEASE ===
TOPIC: Cardiovascular Disease | Coronary Artery Disease
SOURCE_URL: https://www.cdc.gov/heartdisease
Heart disease is the leading cause of death in the US. About 697,000 people in the US die of heart disease each year (1 in 5 deaths).
HEART ATTACK WARNING SIGNS: Chest pain (pressure, squeezing, fullness, or pain lasting >5 min or going away and returning), discomfort in upper body (arms, back, neck, jaw, stomach), shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness.
WOMEN'S SYMPTOMS may differ: unusual fatigue, sleep disturbances, shortness of breath, indigestion, anxiety.
CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY for heart attack symptoms. Do not drive yourself.
CDC RISK FACTORS: High blood pressure, high LDL cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, obesity/overweight, poor diet, physical inactivity, excessive alcohol use.
PRIMARY PREVENTION: Blood pressure target <130/80. LDL-C lowering with statins. Aspirin (only if 10-year CVD risk ≥10% and not at increased bleeding risk). Smoking cessation. Cardiac rehab.

=== CDC: DIABETES TYPE 2 ===
TOPIC: Endocrine Disease | Type 2 Diabetes
SOURCE_URL: https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes
More than 37 million Americans have diabetes (about 1 in 10). 90-95% have type 2. Another 96 million American adults have prediabetes.
CDC DIAGNOSTIC VALUES:
- Fasting blood sugar ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on 2 separate tests
- Random blood sugar ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms
- A1C ≥6.5%
- 2-hour OGTT ≥200 mg/dL
PREDIABETES: A1C 5.7–6.4%, fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL.
MANAGEMENT TARGETS: HbA1c <7% (most adults), Blood pressure <140/90 mmHg, LDL <100 mg/dL.
MEDICATIONS: Metformin (first-line), SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin — also cardioprotective), GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide — also weight loss), sulfonylureas, insulin.
COMPLICATIONS PREVENTION: Annual eye exam (retinopathy), urine albumin (nephropathy), foot exam, lipid panel, dental care.

=== CDC: OPIOID OVERDOSE ===
TOPIC: Emergency | Opioid Overdose
SOURCE_URL: https://www.cdc.gov/drugoverdose
Overdose deaths involving opioids have quadrupled since 1999. More than 75% of drug overdose deaths in 2020 involved an opioid.
SIGNS OF OPIOID OVERDOSE: Unresponsive, slow/shallow/stopped breathing, choking/gurgling sounds, limp body, pale/blue/cold skin, pinpoint pupils.
CDC RESPONSE PROTOCOL: Call 911. Give naloxone (Narcan) if available. Do rescue breathing. Put in recovery position. Stay until help arrives. Give additional naloxone if no response in 2–3 minutes.
NALOXONE DOSING: Nasal spray — 4 mg in one nostril, may repeat in 2–3 min. IM injection — 0.4 mg, may repeat every 2–3 min.
